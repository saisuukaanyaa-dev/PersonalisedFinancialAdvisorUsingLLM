# Risk Profiling Framework

Risk tolerance should be assessed on THREE dimensions, not age alone:

1. **Time horizon** — how far away is the goal?
   - Under 2 years: capital preservation priority (FD, short-term debt funds, liquid funds)
   - 2-5 years: balanced (mix of debt + some equity, bonds)
   - 5+ years: growth priority (equity-heavy — ETFs, equity mutual funds), can absorb volatility

2. **Capacity to bear loss** — income stability, existing emergency fund, dependents, other liabilities. A person with a stable job, adequate emergency fund, and no dependents can take more risk than someone with unstable income and dependents, even at the same age.

3. **Willingness to bear loss** — psychological comfort with volatility. Ask directly rather than assuming.

**Age-based heuristic (starting point only, not a rule):** Equity allocation % ≈ 100 − age. This should be adjusted up or down based on the two factors above and, critically, the actual goal timeline — a 25-year-old saving for a wedding in 18 months should NOT be 75% in equities despite the heuristic suggesting it.

**General allocation bands by profile:**
- Conservative: 10-25% equity, 60-75% debt/FD, 10-15% gold
- Moderate: 40-60% equity, 30-45% debt, 10-15% gold
- Aggressive: 65-85% equity, 10-25% debt, 5-10% gold

These are starting bands for discussion, not prescriptions — always reason from the specific goal, horizon, and capacity/willingness to bear loss.
