# Emergency Fund and Insurance Adequacy

## Emergency Fund
**Rule of thumb:** 6-12 months of essential monthly expenses, held in liquid, low-risk instruments (savings account, liquid mutual funds, short-term FDs with easy premature withdrawal).
- 6 months: stable dual-income household, salaried with job security
- 9-12 months: single income, variable income (freelance/business), dependents, or higher job-market uncertainty

**Should NOT be invested in:** equities, ETFs, long-tenure FDs, or anything with volatility or lock-in — defeats the purpose of instant accessibility.

## Insurance Adequacy

**Term life insurance:** Sum assured typically 10-15x annual income, or enough to cover outstanding liabilities (home loan, etc.) plus future dependent expenses (children's education, spouse's living costs) minus existing assets. Pure term insurance (no maturity payout) is generally more cost-efficient than investment-linked insurance (ULIPs, endowment plans) for pure protection needs — investment and insurance are best kept separate.

**Health insurance:** A base sum insured of at least ₹5-10 lakh per family (India context) is a reasonable starting point, more in metro cities with higher healthcare costs, supplemented by a top-up/super top-up policy for cost efficiency at higher coverage levels. Employer-provided health cover alone is often insufficient since it ends with the job.

**Red flags to surface to the user:**
- No emergency fund at all → highest priority, before any other investment recommendation
- No term life insurance and has dependents → high priority
- Only investment-linked insurance (ULIP/endowment) and no pure term cover → flag the coverage gap
- Emergency fund invested in illiquid/volatile instruments → flag for reallocation
